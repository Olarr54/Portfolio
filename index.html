CASE STUDY: Business Analyst THINKING HYPOTHETICAL

Improving a University Portal Onboarding

PROBLEM

New students consistently miss key deadlines in their first term. The portal exists, but onboarding is assumed, not designed.

USERS

First-year undergrads, international students (higher confusion risk), mature students re-entering education.

PAIN POINTS

Too much information upfront. No clear priority hierarchy. No contextual reminders. Assumes digital confidence.

MY APPROACH

Stakeholder interviews with 5-10 students + a student union rep. Map the actual first week journey. Identify the 3 highest impact touchpoints. Prototype a checklist style onboarding modal progressive disclosure, not an information dump.

WHAT I'D MEASURE

% of students completing key tasks in week 1 before vs. after. Support ticket volume. Time-to-first-login.

ASSUMPTIONS (LABELLED)

I'm assuming the portal has an analytics layer and that the student union would participate. Both need validating.

CASE STUDY: SECURITY AWARENESS REAL LEARNING

Explaining OWASP Top 10 to a Non-Technical Team

THE CHALLENGE

Security training fails when it's technical first. Most employees don't need to understand injection attacks — They need to understand the consequences of clicking the wrong link.

MY APPROACH

Map each OWASP threat to a real world analogy a non-technical person would actually care about. SQL Injection = leaving your front door open but blaming the burglar. Broken Access Control = giving everyone a master key because it's convenient.

WHAT I ACTUALLY KNOW

Studied Kali Linux, Nmap, Wireshark, and Metasploit in my degree. I understand the concepts. I'm not an expert yet but I can explain why they matter and what they protect against.

WHERE I'M HEADING

CompTIA Security+ is the target cert after graduation. I want the formal framework to go with the practical exposure I already have.
